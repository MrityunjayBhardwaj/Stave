## Open Stave

Visit **[stave.live](https://stave.live)**. Stave opens on a **start screen** with the intro video, your recent projects, and ways to start:

| Template | What you get |
|---|---|
| **Full Song** | A finished 48-bar song: drums, bass, keys, a flute hook and a sung line written as notes. The best way to see the studio at work. |
| **Starter** | An outrun groove: drums, bass, pads and lead, with a knob on the bass, a piano roll, a scope, and the Sunset Drive visual behind it. What a first visit opens. |
| **Strudel Sketch** | A short pattern to start writing on. |
| **Hydra Visual** | An audio-reactive Hydra sketch of its own. |
| **Blank** | No files of your own yet. Add them from the sidebar. |

You can also open a project file (`.stave`, or a `.zip` from an older Stave) or a recent project from here. Come back to it any time from **Help › Start Screen**.

<Aside type="tip">
Stave runs best in a Chromium browser (Chrome, Edge, Arc, Brave). It also has a layout for [phones and tablets](/guides/phones-and-tablets/).
</Aside>

## Play something

<Steps>

1. Pick **Full Song**.

2. Click in the code and press **Ctrl+Enter** (**⌘+Enter** on a Mac), or press **Play** in the editor's bar.

3. Change something. For example, change a `gain(0.4)` to `gain(0.8)` and press **Ctrl+Enter** again. The change plays from the next cycle.

4. Press **Ctrl+.** (**⌘+.**) to stop.

</Steps>

## Open the studio

At the bottom of the screen are three tabs:

- **[Timeline](/studio/timeline/)**: the whole song, each track as a row of sections, with a playhead you can click to jump.
- **[Pattern](/studio/pattern/)**: the line under your cursor as a step grid, a piano roll or a chord chart.
- **[Mixer](/studio/mixer/)**: a channel strip for every track.

Edits in any of them are written back into your code. Try it: open the **Mixer**, pull a fader, and watch the `.gain` in the code change.

## Tracks

Each `$:` line is a track, and so is each named part:

```js
$: s("bd*4, ~ cp")            // a track
bass: note("c2 eb2 g2 bb1")   // a track called "bass"
  .s("sawtooth")
```

Every track gets a row on the Timeline, a strip in the Mixer, and its own colour. A `$:` track shows as **Track 1**, **Track 2** and so on, by where it sits in the song. Rename it from its Mixer strip or its Timeline row to give it a name of your own, which is written into your code.

## Add a visual

At the end of a track, add `.viz("name")`:

```js
$: note("c4 e4 g4").s("sawtooth").viz("pianoroll")
```

When you next press **Ctrl+Enter**, a visual opens right under that line, fed by that track alone. Built-in names include `pianoroll`, `scope`, `spectrum`, `spiral`, `pitchwheel` and `wordfall`. Any `.p5`, `.hydra` or `.glsl` file in your project can be used by its name, too. See [Make visuals react](/guides/reactive-visuals/) to write your own.

To put a visual behind your code, use the **set bg ▾** button in the editor's bar, or right-click a visual file in the sidebar and choose **Set as Background**.

## Where your work lives

Stave saves your projects in your browser. There's no account and no server. The **File** menu has:

- **New Project…**, **Open Project…**, **Open Recent**, **Rename Project…**
- **Version History…**: go back to, fork or compare earlier versions
- **Open Project File…** and **Export as .stave**, your audio included
- **Bounce to WAV…**: the mix, or one file per track. See [Bounce and share](/guides/bounce-and-share/).
- **Copy Share Link**: a short link that opens the project anywhere
